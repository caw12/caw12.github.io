From self-portrait to team portrait

This semester in Eller’s Business Communication (BCOM) program I collected data on my values, my communication habits, and how classmates see me. Here is what it showed and what I plan to do about it.

Values that steer my career

I want to build strong relationships with experienced accountants, sharpen my professional and accounting skills, and work somewhere that values helping others. My motivator assessment supports these priorities: financial interest is my strongest area, with stability and financial gain each rated 75 out of 100, and achievement and competition rated 85.

The teammate who builds bridges

The assessment places each person on two scales. Proactivity measures how readily you assert your own thoughts and needs. Reactivity measures how readily you emote and connect with others. My composite scores are -1.0 for proactivity and 2.0 for reactivity, which makes me an “Amiable”: generous with time, motivated by harmony, and attuned to teammates’ feelings. The risk is drifting off task while nurturing relationships, so I keep meetings anchored to an agenda.

Proactivity −1.0Reactivity 2.0Style: Amiable

Shared effort, shared credit

Every member of my team contributed equally and offered constructive feedback to the others. We communicated promptly so that each person felt seen and heard. We assigned each member an individual task, then met frequently to merge our work, which kept our presentations cohesive and our ideas aligned.

Practice over talent

This semester I strengthened my presentation and communication skills. I gained confidence in speaking with colleagues, and I measured progress in specific behaviors: a more engaging vocal tone, a steady posture, purposeful gestures, and appropriate attire. I also learned to guide the audience’s attention to teammates and to speak conversationally rather than from a script.

Identity, brand, and reputation

Identity is how I perceive myself, reputation is how others perceive me, and brand is the deliberate message that connects the two. I regard myself as caring, collaborative, and committed to helping others. My communication assessment supports that view, and my peers’ feedback corroborates it. They consistently describe me as respectful, approachable, and dependable:

“Carter treats others with great respect and is always considerate when working with others.”
“Everyone loves and respects Carter. He is a rockstar who can get anyone to open up to him.”
“Your ability to remain easygoing and easy to talk to while also remaining someone who can be trusted with major responsibility is rare and valuable.”

The data also points to one development area. A peer described me as well-rounded and respectful, and wrote that my professional persona will improve further once I present my ideas more confidently:

“His professional persona will further excel once he presents his ideas and statements more confidently.”

This observation aligns with my lower proactivity score. My brand, then, is that of a trustworthy, relationship-centered accountant, and my priority going forward is to pair that reliability with greater assertiveness, by contributing my analysis earlier in team discussions and volunteering for leadership responsibilities.

Native deodorant: how websites turn quizzes into product choices

Context: My team presented recommendations to a client, Native deodorant, in a client communication presentation for my BCOM course. The group built the full deck. I was responsible for my section on website design and the product quiz, shown above.

Claim: I can research a company’s digital presence, compare it with competitors, and present the findings as clear, evidence-based recommendations for a client.

Reasoning: I benchmarked Native against Head & Shoulders, Carpe, and Dove, examining how each site uses design to feature products, improve accessibility, and deliver a quiz. I found that Native’s quiz hides in a small banner link, returns a scent personality instead of a product, and never asks about skin sensitivity, sweat, or values, while competitors use more precise surveys. Each slide pairs annotated screenshots with a one-line takeaway, which shows I can turn visual research into a message a client can act on.
